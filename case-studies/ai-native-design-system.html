Overview

Building a design system for an AI-native Product Development Life Cycle (PDLC) in the EMR space required changing the design system from a well-documented UI-Kit into a prototyping partner.

I built an intelligent exoskeleton designed to eliminate the manual friction of doc-hunting and boilerplate translation. By automating system compliance, we ensure our human experts spend their time where it matters most: cognitive ergonomics and clinical safety.

Four Technical Layers

I restructured the design system across four distinct technical layers, moving away from static UI kit and toward a dynamic, machine-interpretable stack. This architecture explicitly separates the source of truth from the tooling that humans interact with.

Contracts

This is the foundational database of the design system. It contains no visual assets, only machine-readable logic. A YAML contract for each component was created to represent a source of truth for code, Figma, and any other representation across the suite of products.

Semantic Tokens

Design tokens were converted from a headless, contextless system to semantic naming, to aid LLMs in selecting the right one for every situation.

Selection via MCP

Based on Storybook’s native MCP, I created a number of additional tools to help LLMs convert design intent from prompts into the correct selection for components and tokens. When Storybook builds, a new manifest of component contracts and semantic token naming conventions is built too—keeping the MCP tools updated.

Prototyping

Additional MCP tools provide context to LLMs serving as advanced prototypes, covering basic design rules, preferred layout scaffolding, industry-specific language usage, and more. I have plans to build in user-research to flag incongruent design choices as part of an advanced linting process. Design Systems adherence can merge with general design best-practices.

The Impact on the PDLC

The integration of these tools fundamentally altered our delivery velocity and structural integrity by keeping the AI focused on compliance and humans focused on judgment.

Intent-driven surfacing

Designers and developers no longer have to guess at the component which has the features they want, and AI no longer tries to build it from scratch. This reduces design drift and increases consistency.

Safe agentic prototyping

Product managers, developers, and designers can now use internal agentic tools to rapidly spin up functional, interactive prototypes of new clinical workflows. Because the agents have the right tools and context, these prototypes are instantly compliant with our accessibility and safety standards.

Reclaiming cognitive space

The design system is no longer an additional burden for designers and developers to dread. It’s an active, product building partner.

Design system adherence

The earliest version of these tools cranked adherence from 30% to 91%, capturing all but the most complex use-cases. Measurements will be even higher with the new generation of tools.

Human Judgment over Rote Mechanics

By automating the rote mechanics of design systems—matching tokens, checking color contrast, and writing boilerplate—we’ve given our product teams their time back. A designer no longer spends 40 minutes verifying if a specific button state complies with our internal accessibility standards. They spend that 40 minutes interviewing our users to ensure the workflow actually makes sense in a high-stress hospital environment.
